
Games are nothing without rules, but that doesn’t mean these rules aren’t bendable or even breakable. Through the example of Pong (although it could work with any game), Barr argues that what might seem like a monolithic, completed game with a strict set of rules can actually be changed in any number of ways in order to create new games. It might not even be big changes, one line of code being edited, deleted or added has the ability to radically change the entire experience. The effect of these tweaks is felt while playing the game through the mechanics that break, the types of challenges that arise and the way they make the player feel.

So, this shows that we aren’t bound by the constraints of the games we play. If we’re able to see beneath the surface of the rulebook, we are able to expand our experiences with gaming however we like. One simple idea can be edited any which way to create games that are more fun, or philosophical, nonsensical, etc.

As game designers, this hopefully opens our eyes to the type of creativity that can be applied to our work and to not feel stuck when starting out. Regardless of if you are redesigning an existing game, where the set of rules is clearly written out, or creating something from scratch while still feeling bound to some hidden limitations, sticking to the idea of ‘playful exploration’ is always beneficial. For me, this could definitely help with coming up with ideas for potential games. Rather than going in blind and quickly becoming overwhelmed, it might be easier to find games, art or simply ideas that interest me and slowly, line by line, tweak them until they become their own creation.
