**Brainstorming for Stuck in a loop**
Could be some sort of puzzle game, all the pieces can only be acquired through multiple playthroughs. Don't think too big. One idea, but implement it well.

**Ideas:** 
- Craft the perfect 'something'. Can only accomplish that by crafting lots of wrong things
- Story-based, get through an interaction but you can only know what to say by having experienced the interaction before
- Some sort of dream that repeats
- You gotta work through your thoughts, maybe journaling and looking for clues
- Monotonous day of something, like work
- **Read a bunch of letters, try to figure out something**
	- **Whole game could be like hands holding a pile of memorabilia (photos, letters, etc) and you go to the next and then the next and so on. You have to figure something out from them**

**Chosen idea: Loop through a stack of memorabilia** 
UI based, you are at a desk/library/living room space, looking through a stack of memorabilia (books, images, letters etc)
Main gameplay, click/drag a button to look through the stack
You're trying to uncover something. What? 
Looping has to be part of it. you can't just look through them all once and figure it out, you have to go back (preferably multiple times)
Secondary gameplay: Maybe clicking on the clues means writing a report. (ex, click on name addressed in letter, report gets filled out..)
Maybe you're writing your own letter about your findings. Maybe its like family history, or a missing person, or some sort of relationship. Things that can be inferred through the visuals and content.
More like a visual story, but still with some sort of interactivity that makes the experience entertaining

**Improved idea:** You're a painter trying to make a painting about a scene from your past. You forget details though, so you have to look through memorabilia to remember. You loop again and again to recall more details, which you then add to your painting.
What is the painting of? What is the memorabilia? And the clues to collect?

**Journal Entry 1**
Since this prototype is more story based, I've been spending a lot of time trying to figure out both the overall theme as well as each specific detail (What are the letters/photos of? What clues can be found in them? What does the painting show?). Here is what I've decided on:
You are painting a scene from your childhood, showing you and your two brothers sitting on the steps in front of your childhood home. The stack of memorabilia will consist of 8 items. A map, a drawing of a house, a class photo, a height chard on a doorframe, a diary entry, a postcard, a group photo, a birthday card (might tweak/add more later). As you go through the stack and try clicking on different sections in the stack, thought bubbles will appear on the meaningful ones (as if you are slowly remembering different parts of your childhood). These will have some sort of clue as to what you are looking for (ex. the thought "Our old house... where was it again?" is resolved by clicking on the correct house on the map). Finding and resolving each clue will lead to the next scene in the game, where you paint what you've just uncovered. The game will be split into 3 phases, with a specified theme to the clues you are looking for (ex. phase 1 is location, 2 is people, etc.). After each phase is when you get to paint.

Although this experience may not be very gameplay heavy, I hope to make the story and visuals engaging enough to keep the player entertained. The clues should be intuitive enough so that the player doesn't just mindlessly click every item, but also not too simple as to make it boring. I should also think more about what I want the player to feel. Is it nostalgia? Relatability? Sadness? Or should the experience be closer to that of a cozy game? I'm leaning closer to a low stakes puzzle game, where there is no way to get it wrong but it may just take a while.

Next, I'll work on figuring out the remaining clues. Once they are all implemented (in each phase), I can get started on visuals. The whole game is UI-based, so doesn't require much code. The last step will be adding sounds. These should be minimal, like a page turning or a brush stroke.
