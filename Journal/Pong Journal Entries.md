#### Update #1
My edited version of Pong, Inverse Pong. Originally, my idea was to have a 2 player game of Pong, but every few seconds the up and down keys switch to random keys on the keyboard. My intention was to make the game enter into the physical world, making the players aware of their hands/body while they play. I also thought having two players, instead of playing against the bot, would add even more physicality to the game since the players would have to work with or against each other to access the keys they need to press (instead of keeping to their “side” of the keyboard). I realized thought that this might be a bit too complex, since I would first have to add a second player, a randomized timer, and the actual key switching. Instead, I created this version. Every few seconds, the w and s keys are simply inverted. I also added the “Switch Mode” text in the middle of the screen, which displays for 2 seconds right before the switch happens just to make it a little more clear what’s happening. But what does this actually change about the game? Other than making it a little bit harder? I don’t know.

#### Update #2
2nd version of Pong, building on the last one. Instead of Inverse Pong meaning inverse the up and down keys, this version inverses the players’ sides (through who controls what paddle). The score isn’t swapped. After testing it out, I realize this makes the whole game sort of meaningless, since determining a winner is purely based on luck (whoever switches at the right time to get the higher score). Sort of takes the fun out of it, but still interesting.

#### Update #3
3rd version of Pong. The last version worked as intended but felt unfinished, since the scoring mechanism became somewhat meaningless with constant side switching. If the game were to end, the winner would be determined purely based on luck (on who happened to be on the side with the highest score). To fix this, I changed the core idea of the game to be to get the highest time without missing the ball. Had a lot of issues with the ball movement and speed that I couldn’t fix, so ended up starting from scratch and watching the tutorial video. 

Although I liked my original idea, it became very clear that the idea alone wasn’t enough. The game just wasn’t much of a game anymore and got boring very fast. I thought for a while about what could make it better for the player and what we talked about in class. I decided to go with the time-based approach because it gave the player a new goal not linked to score-keeping. One thing to rework, if I was to keep working on this, is what to do with the computer player. The paddle is needed for the switching mechanism, but the cpu player itself is not. Right now, if the computer misses, the ball just resets. I should think about making it unbeatable, or maybe switching it out with just a wall? An “enemy” just doesn’t feel right for a game like this.
