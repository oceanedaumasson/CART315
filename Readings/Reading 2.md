Schön & Bennett: Reflective Conversation With Materials.
  
In this reading, John Bennett interviews Donald Schön, a Professor of Urban Design at MIT, about the activity of design. This includes ideation, process, the designer’s intention and, throughout all of this, the reflective conversation with materials. Schön states that through creating, a reflection in action takes place where the designer has a sort of back-and-forth with their invention that leads to the finished product. The design-process doesn’t just happen with a designer thinking of an idea and creating it perfectly on the first try. Instead, they try something and see what works, what doesn’t, and what might confuse them. 

Later on, the interview moves from conversation with material to conversation with the user through the invention. By conversation, they don’t mean verbal dialogue. Rather than the user explicitly saying what may or may not be well-designed about the artifact, their response is shared through how their emotions towards it or the alternative and unintentional uses they get from it.

For me, the core idea of what they discussed is that design isn't a linear process. You don’t just come up with an idea and execute it in one go. Creating comes with experimentation, iteration and a lot of thinking. Even more, your intention as a designer is only part of the equation. What seems to you like a simple product with a clear way to go about using it might actually be completely misconstrued by the people it's meant for. This doesn’t mean the invention is bad or was made wrong, just that you don’t truly know what the outcome of your work will be until you put it out there.

Having analysed this, I think it can serve as a good reminder to not try to rush the process or come to a conclusion too fast when I am working on a project. It is more rewarding to slowly work towards a solution that works with me, the material and the user than it is to create something in a short amount of time and then dislike it when I look back on it.
